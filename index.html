M.D. Sky OS

mdskyos.com (opens in new tab) ↗

A dispatch and operations platform for a trucking company. The owner, dispatchers, drivers and customers each sign in to their own interface, working from the same shipment records.

Who sees what — simplified
OwnerEverything; manages users and roles
DispatcherOpen and active loads; assigns drivers
DriverOnly their assigned loads; updates status from a phone
CustomerOnly their own shipments and where each stands
What I did
Designed and built it; configured Google sign-in, the Supabase/PostgreSQL database and the domain and DNS on Cloudflare; ran testing and releases with the owner.
Key decision
Access is enforced where data is read and written, not by hiding menu items — so a driver or customer who edits a URL still only reaches their own records.
Testing
After each change I sign in as every affected role and check what it can do — and what it should be refused. Changes are tracked in Git, so a broken release can be rolled back.
Status
Live: role-based app, sign-in, core dispatch workflows, hosting and domain. In progress: further third-party integrations, including transportation-management, and full rollout with the client’s team.

How I troubleshoot

  1. Scope it

    Who is affected, since when, and what exactly do they see? One user and twenty users are different problems.

  2. Check the layers

    Connectivity, sign-in, permissions, recent changes, and whether the service itself is behaving — in roughly that order.

  3. Test one thing at a time

    Pick the most likely explanation, test it, and change one thing — with a way to undo it.

  4. Verify and write it down

    Confirm with the person who reported it, record the cause and fix, and escalate when it’s beyond my access or role.

This is how I work on the applications I build and support. In a larger IT team, I’d follow its own runbooks, change process and escalation paths.
